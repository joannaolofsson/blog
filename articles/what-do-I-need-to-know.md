### I just discovered what happed to my curiosity, using AI. I have always been a curious person. Ever since I was a small child I asked questions. Back then they came to me naturally and mostly began with the word "Why". It most certainly annoyed the hell out of a lot of people. My dad used to say, "If you don't ask questions, you won't learn anything" or something simular. You might ask, how can you know that this is what he said and the answer is, because I can feel it, It strikes a cord in me.

### As A CBT therapist asking questions is the backbone of the trait. To really tap into a persons story. To understand my clients I needed to ask questions to help them shift perspective, to get new insigts, to feel empowered and to propel them forward. And then I transitioned into development. And all my questions vanished. Why? I felt confused. On paper, I was still a curious person." What happened to all of my questions?". 

### From what I have learned, an indication of what you want out of life, is your answer to question, "Who am I jealous of?". I was always jealous of creators, especially in the tech space. To go to work and get to be both smart and creative was the ultimate job for me. In heinsight, I believe that I had the misconception that having a creative job, meant that you where a really artzy person and that you would probably never get paid. Why did'nt I transition right there and then, you might wonder? And the answer was that I was curious about something else. And that is how I became a therapist. I used to love meeting people all day, listen to their stories and ask them to fill in theirs and my blanks. 

### What sparked my entusiasm for therapy was the moments where I could see a person come to life in our session. For a spark in their eyes, a brief moment that gave away that a new thought or perspective had entered their mind. If you loved it so much, why did you get bored of it? The quickest answer is that I wanted to create my own result. Working as a therapist, meant standing on the sideline and watching other people create results for themself. And I wanted some of that for myself.

### So back to development. Since I began my transition into tech, I have been like a sponge and have tried to take in a stack of the information almost every day. When teachers, supervisors or fellow students hava asked me if I have any questions, my mind has gone blank. And then today, when creating this project as a part of another project, I want to ai to help me plan it. From using ai a while now I have leared that that being very specific in asking your questions, gets you better answers. Just as it is with clients. But instead of just asking away. This time i really thought about what I needed to know learn from the answers. And in that moment, I was the client that got to think a new thought. I then realized that I had been so focused on learning that I have forgotten to put myself in the equation. What do I need to know? What can help me to enhance my skill, my person or both?

### This new insight propelled me forward and suddenly the questions started to poor out of me. And since it was to ai I asked my quesitons I didn't even feel that stupied (just a little). "What files in my project is server side files and which is client-side?" "Can I determine if it is serverside or clientside by looking at the functions, the filestructure or on the imports?" And then I asked myself the question, how come I didn't ask these questions before.

### It was a bit of a revelation becuase i think that I have been totally checked out from one part of my brain and completly checked in to another. I have been totally focused on learning to code, so that I have forgotten my inner child and the creative side of me as well. The one that wants to know why and how. My challenge is now to put it into practice, to be the sponge that takes in the code with a therapeutic twist and a more creative mind. This will mean to habitually ask myself: What do I need to know in order to move this project forward, help its future users and myself. And for my inner dialog I will use, "Is this a dumb question to ask?" And I will answer back to me "The only dumb thing would be to hold your questions in?"
